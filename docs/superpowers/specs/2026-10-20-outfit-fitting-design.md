# Outfit fitting: cloaks that fit every roster body, sit neatly and swing

Date: 2026-10-20. Research behind it: [docs/character/outfit-fitting-research.md](../../character/outfit-fitting-research.md). Current pipeline: [docs/character/outfit-steps.md](../../character/outfit-steps.md).

## Intent

A player can wear an outfit (today: the Hokage and Akatsuki cloaks) over any of the five roster characters (Hero, Bika, Cat, Misha, Pabu) and it looks right:

- **Idle:** the cloak sits neatly on the body. No visual artifacts: nothing of the body (arms, shirt, skin) shows where it should be hidden inside the cloak.
- **Moving (walking, running, jumping):** the hem and sleeves swing and trail behind.

Future outfits will also be things worn over the existing body: other garments (shirts, jumpers) and accessories (sunglasses). The pipeline built here must take a new garment from Tripo to the game without new code.

## Why the current approach fails

Today one cloak mesh is skinned once, to the Hero, by `Scripts/character/skin_outfit.py`, and the game puts that same mesh on every body through leader pose (`AShinobiPlayerCharacter::ApplyOutfit`). Seen in play on the Hero (2026-10-19):

- The hem is skinned partly to the thighs, so a raised knee stretches it into long triangles.
- The script splits each sleeve off the coat below the shoulder so it can follow the arm; with the arm raised the split shows as gaps and holes.
- On the Akatsuki the forearms come out through the sleeve walls.

The bodies also differ too much for one cloak (measured from the rigged FBXs, torso front to back at the chest): Hero 0.23 m, Cat 0.24, Misha 0.26 (hips 0.36), Pabu 0.30, Bika 0.35. A cloak sized to the Hero can't contain Bika or Misha.

## Goals

1. **One fitted copy of each outfit per roster body, generated by script.** The user makes one master garment per outfit (Tripo, then `Scripts/character/prep_outfit.py`, plus `fix_cloak_clouds.py` for the Akatsuki). A script fits a copy to each of the five bodies: sized and shaped to that body (shoulders, chest, hips, arm length, collar round its neck), skinned from that body. Adding a roster character or an outfit means re-running the script, not hand work.
2. **The body under the outfit is hidden.** For each (body, outfit) pair the script also produces a mask of the body surface the garment covers. While that outfit is worn, the game hides those parts of the body. Openings stay visible where the body really shows: the Hokage's open front shows the shirt, the cuffs show the forearms and hands, the collar shows the neck.
3. **Loose parts are simulated cloth.** The hem (skirt) and the sleeves run Chaos Cloth, colliding with the worn body's physics asset, so they swing and trail when moving and come back to rest neatly when the character stops. The shoulders, chest, back and collar stay skinned to the body.
4. **The user tunes the cloth once per outfit, by hand, in Unreal's cloth editor, following full step-by-step instructions** written into `docs/character/outfit-steps.md`: which parts swing and which stay pinned, stiffness, how far the hem and sleeves may travel. The aim is that this tuning applies to all five fitted copies. If the engine can't carry it across cleanly, the fallback is tuning each copy with the same steps; the builder decides which, and the instructions say which.
5. **Tails come out through an opening in the back.** The animal characters keep their tails (`docs/character/model-steps.md`); the Cat's hangs from the hips to about knee height. A tailed body's fitted copy gets an opening in the back that the tail passes through, placed and sized by the fitting script from that body's tail. The tail stays visible; the hem cloth collides with it, so the tail needs collision (it isn't in the ragdoll physics asset today). The opening's edge must look finished, with no torn or ragged cloth around it. A body without a tail gets no opening; the script finds which bodies have one.
6. **The game wears the copy made for the current body.** Changing character or outfit swaps both the garment mesh and the body mask. Picks still replicate exactly as today (`CharacterIndex`, `OutfitIndex`).
7. **Both cloaks are rebuilt this way and pass the check below on all five bodies.**

## Non-goals

- **Chaos Outfit Asset parametric resizing.** It's built around MetaHuman bodies, and per Epic's forum, keeping the cloth simulation through a resize isn't solved.
- **Mutable.**
- Outfits on enemies or villagers.
- Synchronising cloth over the network: each machine simulates its own.
- Proving the pipeline on a close-fitting garment (shirt, jumper) now. The fitter and the mask stay general enough for one, but only the two cloaks are built and tested; the user will bring a real shirt or jumper later.
- Building an accessory (sunglasses) now. Nothing in the design should stop one being added later as a rigid mesh attached to the head and fitted per body.
- Wearing several items at once (a jumper under a cloak, sunglasses with a cloak). One outfit slot, as today. *(My call: the user named future garments and sunglasses but not combining them; slots are a later change.)*

## Anti-goals: it has failed if any of these show, even if it technically works

- Stretched strands or triangles at the hem, armpits or sleeves in any pose.
- Holes or tears under the arms or anywhere else in the garment.
- Body parts visible through the garment: idle, walking, running, jumping or casting.
- See-through gaps: looking through an opening or a gap in the cloth and seeing the background where a hidden body part should be.
- Cloth that keeps jiggling, sagging or drifting after the character stops.
- A cloak that only works on the Hero.

## Constraints

- Unreal Engine 5.8 (`C:\Program Files\Epic Games\UE_5.8`). Chaos Cloth, the Chaos Cloth Asset editor and Dataflow nodes, Chaos Outfit Asset and Mutable all ship with it.
- Blender 5.2 headless (`"C:\Program Files\Blender Foundation\Blender 5.2\blender.exe" -b -P ...`) for the mesh pipeline, as for the other `Scripts/character/*.py`.
- Roster bodies: `SourceArt/Character/Models/<Name>/<Name>_rigged.fbx` for Hero, Bika, Cat, Misha and Pabu. The user rigged them on Mixamo; each is the 65-bone Mixamo skeleton, but each sits slightly differently in its FBX (hence the hips offset in `ApplyOutfit`). They are imported by `Scripts/setup_hero.py` to `/Game/Shinobi/Hero/SK_Hero` and `/Game/Shinobi/Characters/<Name>/SK_<Name>`. Each body plays Manny's animations through an IK retargeter (`UShinobiBodyComponent::Wear`). Bodies ragdoll on death, so they have physics assets; the builder should check those suit cloth collision.
- Body material: instances of `M_Character`, already Masked (opacity from the texture's alpha) and two-sided. A body-hiding mask can feed its opacity.
- The outfit follows the body through leader pose on `AShinobiPlayerCharacter::OutfitMesh`, and is outlined with the same custom-depth stencil as the body (`ApplyOutlineFor`).
- Imports run with the Unreal Editor closed (`Scripts/run_python.ps1 -Script Scripts/setup_hero.py`, `SETUP_HERO_ONLY=outfits` for outfits only). Never switch or merge branches with uasset changes while the editor is open.
- `SourceArt/Character/Outfits/<Name>/` only commits the outputs (`*_outfit.fbx`, `*_outfit.png`, Git LFS); intermediates stay local. The new per-body outputs need matching `.gitignore` rules.
- Tests: `Scripts/run_tests.ps1 -Filter ShinobiCoop.Hero`. `OutfitFollowsBodyAndComesOff` must keep passing and be extended to the per-body copies. Per the user's standing instruction, test only what changed during the work, and run the full suite once at the end.
- Outfit references the user made: `SourceArt/Character/Reference/Cloak_<name>_{front,back,left,right}.png` (not committed). Tripo exports: `SourceArt/Character/Outfits/<Name>/<name>_cloak.zip`.

## What's decided about the how

- **One master garment per outfit, fitted per body by script** (goal 1). Not one mesh stretched over every body; not parametric resizing.
- **Body hiding by mask** (goal 2), per (body, outfit) pair, produced by the fitting script and applied in the game when the outfit is worn.
- **Chaos Cloth for hem and sleeves; skinned upper body** (goal 3). The current tricks in `skin_outfit.py` that existed only to stop rigid skinning from tearing (splitting sleeves off the coat, dropping weld slivers, evening weights across layers, easing the legs' pull on the skirt) are expected to be unnecessary for simulated parts. Removing or keeping them is the builder's call, judged by the check below.
- **Manual cloth tuning by the user in the Unreal cloth editor**, with complete step-by-step instructions (goal 4).
- **The check, run for both cloaks on all five bodies:**
  1. Blender posed renders of each fitted copy over its body (idle A-pose, arms down, arms out, arms up, jump tuck), checking the skinned parts, the mask and the stretched-edge count. A posed-render script was used during the 2026-10-19 work: rotate pose bones in armature space, count edges stretched more than 3x.
  2. In-game screenshots of each body in each cloak: idle, walking, running and mid-jump, from the front, the side and behind. These judge the cloth and the mask together.
  3. The user's own play-test is the final say.

## Calls made without the user (check these)

- **Start from the current master garments.** The 2026-10-19 Tripo Hokage and the regenerated Akatsuki (sleeves about 21 degrees out) are the masters. If one can't be made to pass the check, for example because its sleeves can't separate from the coat for cloth, the builder writes a reference spec and asks the user to regenerate it in Tripo, rather than doing heavy mesh surgery. `fix_cloak_clouds.py` is re-run on any new Akatsuki master.
- **Working cloth defaults ship first.** The build delivers a scripted default cloth setup (which parts swing, how far, stiffness, collision) that already passes the check. The user's hand tuning (goal 4) refines it; the step-by-step instructions describe changing those defaults, not starting from nothing. If part of the cloth setup can't be scripted in 5.8, the instructions cover doing that part by hand, and the plan says where it stops for the user.

## Left to the builder

- **Which Unreal cloth path:** the legacy clothing tool in the skeletal mesh editor (paint a cloth section) or the Chaos Cloth Asset with Dataflow. Also how the user's one tuning reaches all five copies (Dataflow transfer, a scripted weight map, or per-copy tuning as the documented fallback), and whether cloth on a leader-pose follower simulates as needed. Verify in 5.8 before committing to a path.
- **The fitting method:** landmark scaling, shrink-wrap to the body with clearance, a deformation driven by matching body landmarks, or a mix. It must keep the garment's own look (folds, collar shape, clouds and kanji placement) while containing each body.
- **How the mask is computed and stored** (body texture-space mask, per-vertex, or per-section), and how the game applies it to the body's materials.
- **Collision setup per body** (physics asset bodies used for cloth, extra capsules for thighs and arms if needed), simulation distance and LOD limits, and how cloth settles at idle.
- **Where per-body outputs live** (paths, asset names, the `Outfits` table shape in `ShinobiPlayerCharacter.h`), and how `setup_hero.py` imports them.
- **What happens to `skin_outfit.py`:** evolve it into the per-body fitter or replace it.
- **The order of work**, including whether to prove the approach on one cloak and one body before scaling to ten.
